Educators & Leaders

Of all the things a school controls, nothing shapes how much a child learns more than the teacher at the front of the room and the principal down the hall.

— The Wexmoor Foundation Impact Plan

Research consistently finds that teachers are the most important in-school influence on student achievement, and that school leaders play a critical role as well, especially in keeping excellent teachers. Principals create the conditions in which great teaching and learning can happen.

Our goal is to invest in high-performing, results-focused, sustainable programs and strategies that advance these goals for teachers and leaders:

  • Recruiting the most qualified candidates into the profession
  • Developing current educators so they can better help students achieve
  • Retaining the most effective educators

Outcomes Sought

We support organizations that measure their results and use evidence-based strategies. Ultimately, we want to see growth in student achievement as a result of excellent teachers and leaders. Our Outcomes Sought document summarizes our priorities and serves as a basis for conversation with grantees.

Strategic Issue: Strong Teachers for Every Classroom

Teachers are the most important school-based factor in a student’s academic success. There are outstanding teachers in every North Carolina district. Yet students from low-income families and students of color are less likely to be taught by the most effective teachers, whether across schools or within them.

Our goal is simple: increase the likelihood that every student is taught by a highly effective teacher.

Strategies

The Wexmoor Foundation is committed to finding and advancing ways to grow the pool of well-prepared new teachers and to multiply the reach of highly effective teachers.

Grow the Pipeline

Growing the pool of effective new teachers by:

  • Strengthening clinical experiences, including residencies, for teacher candidates who are likely to be hired into and succeed in high-poverty schools
  • Building shared ownership between school districts and educator preparation programs

Multiply the Reach

Multiplying the reach of highly effective teachers so that principals are:

  • Using effectiveness data to match teachers with the students who need the most support
  • Adopting staffing models in which excellent teachers lead small teams and reach more students

Additional Information

The Foundation dedicates time and advocacy, its work beyond grantmaking, to this strategic issue. The grantmaking focus areas of Early Grades Achievement and Educators & Leaders, adopted by the Board in 2014, remain unchanged, and the Foundation’s focus areas also include extracurricular activities. Read Brannock, launched with Foundation support in 2016, and early reading proficiency remain a top priority and strategic issue for the Foundation.

Data That Inspires Our Approach

Why we need to grow the pipeline

  • Brannock County Schools employs roughly 2,100 classroom teachers. Each year, about one in six classrooms in the district’s highest-poverty schools is led by a teacher in their first three years.
  • Beginning teachers are more likely to be hired late in the summer and placed in higher-poverty schools, where turnover is also highest.

Why we need to multiply the reach

  • When excellent teachers lead small teams of colleagues, more students experience top-quartile instruction.
  • In Brannock County Schools, 41% of students in low-poverty schools were taught by a teacher who exceeded expected growth the prior year, compared with 24% of students in high-poverty schools.
